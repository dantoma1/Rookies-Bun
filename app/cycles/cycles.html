The Rookie Algorithm

Four things we read. Most platforms read one.

You're more than a CV. A role is more than a job description. The Rookie Algorithm looks at four things every other platform misses — and weighs them against everyone else in the cycle, not against an empty queue.

Layer 01

What you can do

Your education, projects, experience, skills, languages and work authorisation. Read fast, read fair, read the same for everyone. Big brand, startup, SMe — same score.

Layer 02

Who you are

The things that don't fit a checkbox. Drive, ownership, the depth of the project you actually built, the voice in your story. Read by the algorithm and surfaced with a one-line reason.

Layer 03

The cycle around you

Fit isn't an absolute number. It depends on who else showed up. The Rookie Algorithm clears the whole market at once — not your application against a void.

Layer 04

The Rookies cohort

Every cycle teaches the next. Students stay on as alumni, employers refine their pool, universities see what actually happens after graduation. Match once and you're a Rookie for good.

Why a cycle, not a board

A board is a feed. A cycle is a moment.

Job boards sell ads and reward scrolling. They show you the loudest companies, not the right ones. A cycle inverts the architecture: everyone in the same room, the Rookie Algorithm running once, five matches each — done.

Today · Job boards

Spray, pray, repeat.

  • 100 applications. 1 interview. If you're lucky.
  • The same five big brands collect 2,000 CVs each; the right-fit sits empty
  • Ranked by ad spend, not by you
  • Two sides drift in and out on different clocks — never quite meeting
  • "Why didn't I match?" → "Unfortunately (not really), your profile was impressive (we barely opened it), we're moving forward with others (anyone, really), and we'll keep your CV on file (we will not).
Rookies cycles

Five matches. Real interviews.

  • Five matches per student, ranked by actual fit
  • SMEs and scale-ups compete with big brands on signal, not ad budget
  • Every match is explained — a tailored, evidence-backed reason you and the job genuinely fit.
  • Both sides show up at the same time. That's why the market clears.
  • The Rookie Algorithm — built on your profile, not your network

The Rookie Algorithm

Four signals we rank on. Job boards rank on none.

Job boards sort candidates by who clicked first and who paid for the boost. The Rookie Algorithm reads four signals from every candidate in the pool and ranks them against your role — so your top five aren't the five who happened to apply, they're the five who actually fit.

Signal 01

Technical fit

Coursework, projects, internships, skills stack, languages and work authorisation — scored against the specific job you posted. No keyword bias, no university-prestige weighting, no inflated CV claims.

Signal 02

Culture fit

The things a CV can't carry. Ownership, depth of the project they actually built, how they describe their work. Surfaced as a one-line reason — so you know why this candidate fits before the first call.

Signal 03

Market-clearing context

A "great candidate" depends on the pool. The Rookie Algorithm clears the whole cycle at once, so your shortlist reflects the strongest five available in this market — not five who happened to click on Tuesday.

Signal 04

Mutual intent

Every candidate on your shortlist already ranked your company in their top ten. No more chasing dream candidates who'll ghost the offer — the interest is mutual before reveal day.

Why a cycle, not a CV pile

A CV pile is noise. A cycle is signal.

Job boards bury you under 300 CVs that mostly don't fit. You spend a week reading the same template motivation copy-pasted by people who'd never heard of you. A cycle inverts the architecture: the whole pool is read for you, and you get a pre-ranked shortlist of five — done.

Today · Job boards

300 CVs. Five worth reading.

  • One listing pulls 300 applications. 295 are noise. The 5 good ones get buried.
  • Same big-brand candidates everyone chases — and same ghosting back from them
  • Motivation letters copy-pasted from a template. No signal.
  • Listings ranked by post recency, not by fit to your role
  • You compete with big brands on ad spend. Never on signal.
